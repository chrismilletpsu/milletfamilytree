# Millet Genealogy — Research Notes

Compiled from the sources cited under each person. Every fact below is tied to a record or publication; where a link between people is inferred rather than documented it is marked **probable** and the reasoning is given. The interactive tree (`index.html`) is built from the same data. Phase 1 (August–September 2026) built the five lines; Phase 2 (September 6–7, 2026) proved or tested the weak links, took the Moyer line back three generations, and filled in the direct-line women. A log of Phase 2 is at the end.


## The questions this research set out to answer

1. **Was an ancestor involved in "cracking" the Liberty Bell?** The lore is half right. John Jacob Mickley Jr. (1737–1808) of Egypt, Whitehall Township, hauled the State House bell out of Philadelphia in September 1777 so the British could not melt it into cannon. His wagon broke down under the bell's weight at Bethlehem on September 23 (the Moravian diary records it), and a neighbor, Frederick Leaser, finished the haul to Zion Reformed Church in Allentown, where the bell was hidden under the floor until June 1778. Nothing in any account connects the Mickleys with the crack, which dates from the 1840s. Mickley's eleven-year-old son rode on the wagon.

2. **Are Michelet, Mickley, and Millet the same family?** Michelet and Mickley are: Jean Jacques Michelet, born at Zweibrücken in 1697 to a Huguenot family from Metz, landed at Philadelphia in 1733 and his name became Mickley in Pennsylvania. Millet is a different family that married into the Mickley line: Catharine Moyer, granddaughter of Johann Peter Mickley through Susanna Mickley Moyer, married Frederick Millet of Orwigsburg about 1862. The Millets were Millot, Mylot, or Miloth in every record before the 1840s; the resemblance to the French painter's surname is a later spelling and, as far as the records show, a coincidence (see below).

3. **The Millet line above Frederick (1820–1864).** Reconstructed from deeds, tax lists, militia rolls, and newspapers rather than from any single genealogy. See the Millet / Millot section. Phase 2 searched FamilySearch's full-text index of Berks and Schuylkill deeds, probate, and court dockets and found nothing new; the links from Sgt. Frederick to his supposed children and from Martin to Frederick remain probable, and the records that would decide them are named under Open leads.

4. **Christine Nickerson's side.** Traced from Bayonne back through Coeymans, New York, and Ridgefield, Connecticut, to William Nickerson (1604–1690), founder of Chatham on Cape Cod, and now one generation further to Anne Busby's parents, Nicholas and Bridget Busby, who crossed with the family in 1637; and on the Christoffersen side to Denmark and to Egersund, Norway, where Olava Olsdatter's parentage is now proved from the parish registers and censuses.

5. **The Moyer line.** Thomas Moyer (1804–1869), husband of Susanna Mickley, is now tied by his Egypt Reformed baptism and a Lehigh County Orphans' Court guardianship of 1818 to Jacob Meyer (1781–bef. 1818) and Christina Newan of South Whitehall, and through the FamilySearch tree to Peter Meyer Sr. (c. 1752–1838) and Catharina Kern, with Martin Meyer Sr. (1730–1807) above them marked probable.

6. **The women of the direct line.** Four great-grandmothers and grandmothers who had been names with dates now have parents and records: Mary Catherine Martin (1891–1971) of Grier City, daughter of Adolph Martin, a German immigrant of 1881, and Catharine Herring; Marie Violet Williams (1922–2004), daughter of William Earl Williams of Gilberton, with Evelyn Beck identified as her stepmother rather than her mother; Julia Baran (b. 1907), daughter of George and Mary Baran, Slovak immigrants in Bayonne; and Louise Sophie "Lulu" Mihm (1890–1977), daughter of Joseph Mihm and Louisa Mayer of Jersey City.

## How each line is established

**Michelet of Metz.** The 1893 *Genealogy of the Mickley Family of America* prints a family table of the Michelets of Metz sent from Berlin in 1883 by Prof. Karl Ludwig Michelet, a descendant of the emigrant's younger brother. The surviving Reformed registers of Metz (FamilySearch, "France, Protestant Church Records, 1536–1902") confirm that table generation by generation: Jacquemin's marriage to Suzanne Wiriot (1591), Daniel's baptism (1592) and marriage to Marie Colin (1615), Jacques's marriage to Anne Philpin (1646) and burial (1685), and Louis's baptism (17 December 1675, parents "Jacq. Michelet" and "A. Philpin"). Two corrections to what circulates online came out of this: Jacques's wife was Anne Philpin, not "Anne Du Bois" (the Berlin table lists an Anne "veuve Dubois" in his household, which is where the name came from), and the emigrant was born at Zweibrücken because his Protestant parents could not marry in French Metz after 1685. The Zweibrücken registers themselves are not indexed online; the Berlin cousin already warned in 1883 that they were probably destroyed under Louis XIV. The Michelets of Berlin (silk manufacturers, then the philosopher Karl Ludwig Michelet) and the Norwegian Michelets (army officers descended from two brothers who left Metz in 1644) are collateral branches.

**Mickley of Lehigh County.** The 1893 genealogy, the Wikipedia articles on the settler and the soldier, the Zion Reformed Church (Allentown) account of the bell, Find a Grave, and FamilySearch tree profiles with attached records. Peter Mickley's eleven children and Susanna Mickley Moyer's move to Tamaqua come from the genealogy and from the Egypt Reformed Church baptisms.

**Moyer.** Thomas Moyer was born 19 November 1804 in Whitehall Township and baptized at Egypt Reformed Church on 26 January 1805 as Thomas Mayer, son of Jacob Mayer and Christina (indexed baptism). The link to that family is the Lehigh County Orphans' Court docket for February term 1818 (p. 207, no. 14, read from the page image): Christina Moyer, widow of Jacob Moyer of South Whitehall Township, yeoman, petitioned for guardians for her minor children, and Peter Moyer was appointed for Thomas and Stephen. Thomas married Susanna Mickley in South Whitehall in 1833; the 1850 and 1860 Tamaqua censuses (household 80 in the east ward; household 944, page 125) give the nine children, his trade as a potter in 1850, and his office as high constable of the borough in 1860, with two sons working as moulders. Both censuses understate his age by about eight years, which is why the guardianship record, not the FamilySearch tree's match alone, is relied on for his parentage. Jacob Meyer's baptism (Egypt Reformed, 2 September 1781, sponsors Jacob and Gertrude Kern) and the generations above him, Peter Meyer Sr. and Catharina Kern and then Martin Meyer Sr., come from FamilySearch tree profiles and are not yet independently verified; Martin is marked probable. Their daughter Catharine (1833–1925) married Frederick Millet about 1862.

**Millet / Millot.** Frederick Millet (1820–1864) is documented at St. John's Reformed, Orwigsburg (deacon, 1846), in the 1850 and 1860 censuses at Tamaqua, and in Schuylkill County letters of administration of 18 July 1864 and deeds of 1865. His descent from Martin Millot of Orwigsburg rests on the 1830 census household (a boy aged 10–14) and the FamilySearch tree; no baptism has been found, since St. John's register begins in 1833. Martin and John Millot are tied together by deeds of 1816–17 for lot 201 on Perry Street, and to Berks County by the rarity of the name: the only Millot in the Berks tax lists of 1767–1785 is "Mylot, Fred'k, taylor" of Colebrookdale Township (1781), who is the Sgt. Frederick Millet of the 1780 and 1782 militia rolls (both companies were raised from the Colebrookdale–Oley district). Four people of the name born 1777–c. 1785 in the county — Maria and George of Oley, John and Martin of Orwigsburg — are treated as his probable children. Newspapers (Berks and Schuylkill Journal, 1818–1820) add John's sheriff's sales and his role as an Independent Republican committeeman in 1820. In September 2026 FamilySearch's full-text index of Berks and Schuylkill deeds, probate, and court dockets was searched for Millot, Milot, Mylot, and Miloth: it returned only the Orwigsburg deeds already known, a 1817 court docket naming John Millot, a 1856 naturalization of an unrelated Martin Millot, and the 1875 estate of Mary Millot (Joseph's wife), and no Frederick Millot under any spelling. Charles A. Millet's 1911 marriage register entry (p. 385) and his son's 2001 obituary add the Martin family.

**Nickerson.** From Willard (1927–2011) back through Stephen John (b. 1900, Coeymans), Levi (1865–1926), Stephen Smith (1833–1908), Levi (1763–1847), Eliphaz (1739–1794), James (1704–1757), Ensign William (1668–1742), William Jr. (1646–1719), to William Nickerson (1604–1690) of Norwich and Chatham, and to Anne Busby's parents Nicholas and Bridget Busby. Censuses, Find a Grave, FamilySearch, the Nickerson Family Association's history articles (the 2013 "William and Anne (Busby) Nickerson Come to Town," drawing on William C. Smith's *History of Chatham*, and Debra Lawless's 2017 "Our Ancestors' Fascinating Journey" series), and Willard's 2011 obituary. The Association's articles supply the 1637 voyage on the *Rose* or the *John and Dorothy*, the 1656 purchase of Monomoit from Mattaquason and its price, the 1672 settlement with Plymouth Colony for £90, the homestead at Ryder's Cove about 1664, the church services in William's house, and the incorporation of Chatham on 11 June 1712. The Jersey Journal (1942–1954) adds addresses, the 1948 engagement to Evelyn Christoffersen, and the 1954 marriage to Ethel Paul. The 1940 Bayonne census shows Orpha Nickerson remarried as Orpha Kerr, with her son Thomas Kerr, which explains the half-brother named in Willard's obituary.

**Christoffersen / Olsen.** Walter Alvin Christoffersen (b. 1900, Chicago) was the son of Martin Christoffersen, born January 1864 in Denmark to Hans Jacob and Karen (parish not yet found), and Inger Mathilde Olsen, born 25 December 1868 at Egersund, Norway, who married in Chicago on 7 November 1891. Inger's parents Otto Johan Olsen (b. 8 July 1845) and Olava Olsdatter (m. 18 October 1866), and Otto's parents Ole Andreas Olsen and Inger Marie Thorsdatter, come from the Egersund parish registers. Olava's parentage, which Phase 1 could only call probable, is now proved from nine Digitalarkivet records read directly: her baptism of 25 February 1849 (born 25 January, a twin, illegitimate, mother Marthe Olsdatter, a servant girl at Dyrnæs, who named Ole Olsen of Egersund as father), her confirmation of 4 October 1863 giving the same birth date and both parents, the 1865 census with her mother in Storgaden, the 1866 marriage naming her father, the baptisms of her children Ole Ingeman (1867), Ingeborg Andrea (1871), and Marthe (1874), and the 1870 and 1875 censuses with Marthe Olsdatter in the household, "supported by her son-in-law Otto Joh. Olsen" in 1875. Ole Olsen and Marthe never married. On the Jersey City side, Louise S. Mihm is identified with Sophie L. "Lulu" Mihm, born June 1890 to Joseph Mihm and Louisa Mayer, through the 1900 census (with her grandfather Jacob Mayer in the household), the 1922 city directory, her Social Security death record (b. 14 June 1890, d. September 1977), the 1950 census (widowed), and the Find a Grave index (Belvidere Cemetery).

**Martin, Williams, Baran, Mihm.** The four direct-line women researched in Phase 2 are established as follows. Mary Catherine Martin: the Pennsylvania delayed birth record (18 August 1891, Grier City, parents Adolph Martin and Catherine Herring), the 1900 and 1910 censuses of Rush Township, and the 1911 marriage register with her father's consent. Marie Violet Williams: her Social Security NUMIDENT record (parents William E. Williams and Evelyn "Berk"), the 1930 Gilberton census in which she and her brother are grandchildren in William H. and Cora Williams's household, the 1930 Allentown marriage of William E. Williams and Evelyn D. Beck (naming his parents William H. Williams and Cora Dornsife and hers, George A. and Eleanor Dutes Beck), the 1940 and 1950 Tamaqua censuses, his 1942 draft registration, and the Find a Grave entries at Highland Memorial Park. Because Evelyn was single in April 1930 and married in September, she is Marie's stepmother; the birth mother is unidentified. Julia Baran: the 1910, 1915, and 1920 Bayonne censuses (George and Mary Baran, from Hungary/Austria, with ten children), the 1930, 1940, and 1950 censuses, her daughter's NUMIDENT record, and a 1956 residence entry. Louise Mihm: as above.

## Michelet / Mickley

### Jehan Michelet, “le Masson” (fl. 1444)

**Place:** Metz  
**Role:** Earliest recorded Michelet of Metz  
**Generation:** 13  
**Status:** direct ancestor of Chris Millet; link marked probable

A mason (“le Masson”) of Metz recorded in 1444, the head of the genealogical table of the Michelet family of Metz that Prof. Karl Ludwig Michelet of Berlin compiled from city baptismal certificates, the researches of the Metz Protestant pastor, and family papers, and sent to Pennsylvania in 1883. Later generations of the table are borne out by the surviving Reformed registers; the earlier ones, in the words of the 1893 genealogy, are “more or less conjectural.” The same table names a Jean Michelet, butcher, invested with the Order of Saint Michael by Louis XI in 1471 and Seigneur de Beauval, and an Antoine Jacques Michelet, president of the Parlement of Rouen in 1596, bearing the same arms. The family's arms, as carried from Metz to Berlin, show a fortress tower topped by a cap of liberty with a stag leaping the moat, and the motto “War, the Chase and Liberty.”

- Descendant, intermediate generations omitted: Jacques “Jacquemin” Michelet (probable)

### Jacques “Jacquemin” Michelet (c. 1550–1610)

**Place:** Metz  
**Role:** Clerk to the King's treasurer at Metz  
**Generation:** 12  
**Status:** direct ancestor of Chris Millet

A scrivener (escripvain) of Metz in 1576, clerk in 1579, receiver (receveur) in 1587, and from 1593 clerk to the King's treasurer; died in 1610. Twice married in the Reformed church: first to Suzanne Joly, who died in 1584, and on July 28, 1591, to Suzanne Wiriot. By his first wife he had Jacques (1576–1651, an elder of the church), Susanne, Pierre (1582–1632, a pastor), Paul (1584, deacon and farmer of the city mills, whose sons went to Norway), and Daniel; by the second, Samuel and Esther, who married David de la Cloche.

### Paul Michelet (1584)

**Place:** Metz → (sons to Norway, 1644)  
**Role:** Ancestor of the Norwegian Michelets  
**Generation:** 11  
**Status:** relative

Baptized December 16, 1584, son of Jacquemin Michelet and Suzanne Joly; a deacon of the Reformed church and farmer of the city mills of Metz. Married Anne Pillon, daughter of Josué Pillon, receiver of the bullette tax. Two of his sons, Paul (baptized September 5, 1617) and Jacques (baptized April 7, 1619), emigrated to Norway in 1644 as officers in the Danish-Norwegian army; Paul was a lieutenant in 1644 and major by 1658, present at the siege of Trondheim, and died in 1659. - Children: Paul (1617–1659), Jacques (1619)

### Jacques Michelet (1623–1685)

**Place:** Metz, Lorraine  
**Role:** Dyer and deacon of the Reformed church of Metz  
**Generation:** 10  
**Status:** direct ancestor of Chris Millet

Born in Metz and baptized in its Reformed church on December 28, 1623, son of Daniel Michelet and Marie Colin. A dyer (teinturier) by trade and a deacon of the Protestant congregation. Married Anne Philpin at Metz on October 30, 1646; the family table compiled in Berlin carries an entry for an Anne, died 1681, “veuve Dubois,” which is probably how the name Du Bois came to be attached to her in some later trees. Metz had been a free city of the Holy Roman Empire until France seized it in 1552, and its large Reformed community kept its own registers from 1561 until the Revocation. Anne died in 1681. Jacques was buried on October 8, 1685, days before the Edict of Fontainebleau outlawed Protestant worship in France that same month. Of his children, Pierre became a mercer and deacon; the youngest, Louis, born when his father was fifty-two, carried the line to Zweibrücken and America.

- Parents: Daniel Michelet
- Children (linked in tree): Louis Michelet
- Spouse: Anne Philpin (c. 1630–1681), m. 30 Oct 1646
- Children: Anne (1649), Pierre (1652), Jacques (1657), Judith (1659), Louis (1675)

### Louis Michelet (1675–1750)

**Place:** Metz → Zweibrücken  
**Role:** Merchant, later Protestant pastor  
**Generation:** 9  
**Status:** direct ancestor of Chris Millet

Born in Metz on December 17, 1675, and baptized in the Reformed church there, the youngest child of Jacques Michelet and Anne Philpin; he was nine when the Revocation of the Edict of Nantes closed the Metz temple. A merchant. Because no Protestant marriage could be solemnized in French Metz, he married Suzanne Mangeot (born June 26, 1674) in 1697 at Zweibrücken (Deux-Ponts), the Palatine duchy just over the border, and the couple stayed there until their first son was born and baptized; Suzanne's own diary, still kept by the Berlin branch of the family in 1883, records this. The Metz archives list Louis and Suzanne in 1699 as “new converts” openly declaring for the Reformed faith, and in 1715 Louis, by then a widower, declaring his five surviving children Protestant. He later served as a Protestant pastor at Zweibrücken and died there on February 27, 1750. His children scattered with the Huguenot diaspora: Jean Jacques to Pennsylvania, Marie and Louis to Berlin, Jeanne out of Metz in 1708 in the household of the Countess of Nassau.

- Parents: Jacques Michelet
- Children (linked in tree): Louis Michelet; Jean Jacques Michelet (John Jacob Mickley)
- Spouse: Suzanne Mangeot (1674–bef. 1715), m. 1697 at Zweibrücken
- Children: Jean Jacques (1697), Jeanne (1699), Barbe (1702), Marie (1703, to Berlin, m. Pierre Perrin), Louis (1705–1766, to Berlin), Pierre (1710)

### Jean Jacques Michelet (John Jacob Mickley) (1697–1769)

**Place:** Zweibrücken → Oley, Berks Co. → Egypt, Whitehall Twp., PA  
**Role:** Immigrant, 1733  
**Generation:** 8  
**Status:** direct ancestor of Chris Millet

Eldest son of Louis Michelet and Suzanne Mangeot, born at Zweibrücken in 1697 while his parents were there to marry; his baptism has never been found, and his Berlin cousin wrote in 1883 that the Palatinate's Protestant records were probably destroyed in the French devastations under Louis XIV. Sailed from Rotterdam on the ship Hope of London, Daniel Reid master, by way of Cowes, and landed at Philadelphia on August 28, 1733, one of 389 Palatine passengers; the list spells him Johan Jacob Mückli, and he took the oath of allegiance the same week. A single man on arrival, he lived several years with Jacob Levan, a Huguenot relative, in the Oley valley of Berks County, then settled at Egypt in Whitehall Township, now Lehigh County, buying land from Adam Deshler by deed of December 14, 1761, on a tract William Penn had first granted in 1681. The name passed through Michelet, Miquelet, Mückli, and Michley before settling as Mickley; the village of Mickleys stands on his original tract, 450 acres of which were still in the family in 1893. Two of his children, Henry and Barbara, were killed in a raid on October 8, 1763. Died August 18, 1769; his wife died the same month. Their slate stones having worn away, descendants reinterred them at St. John's Union (Mickleys) Cemetery under a monument raised by subscription in 1864.

- Parents: Louis Michelet
- Children (linked in tree): John Martin Mickley; John Peter Mickley; Henry Mickley; Barbara Mickley; Pvt. John Jacob Mickley Jr.
- Spouse: Elizabeth Barbara Burkhalter (1719–1769)
- Children: John Jacob (1737), Mary Magdalena (1744), John Martin (1745), John Peter (1752), Henry (1754), Barbara (1756), Susanne

### Louis Michelet (1705–1766)

**Place:** Metz → Berlin  
**Role:** Silk manufacturer in Berlin  
**Generation:** 8  
**Status:** relative

Born at Metz December 8, 1705, younger brother of the emigrant to Pennsylvania. Left Metz for Berlin in 1720, arriving with David Girard, a kinsman by marriage, to apprentice in the silk manufactory of Girard, Michelet & Compagnie, in which he later became a partner; the firm stayed in the family for three generations. Berlin's French colony, founded by Huguenot refugees after 1685, was where the Michelets of Prussia married only within the faith. Died 1766. His son Pierre (1741–1787) was an officer of hussars under Frederick the Great; his grandson Edouard a captain in the war of 1813–15; his great-grandson the philosopher Karl Ludwig Michelet.

- Parents: Louis Michelet
- Descendant, intermediate generations omitted: Prof. Karl Ludwig Michelet
- Children: Louis (1736–1800), Marie Françoise (1738–1796), Pierre (1741–1787), Robert David (1744–1802)

### Pvt. John Jacob Mickley Jr. (1737–1808)

**Place:** Egypt, Whitehall Twp., Lehigh Co., PA  
**Role:** Hauled the Liberty Bell, 1777  
**Generation:** 7  
**Status:** direct ancestor of Chris Millet

Born December 17, 1737. Married Susanne Margarethe Miller in 1760. Elected to the Northampton County Committee of the Revolution in November 1776, and gave the use of his horses and wagons to the Continental Army throughout the war. In September 1777, after the Battle of Brandywine, the Executive Council ordered Philadelphia's bells out of the city so the British could not melt them into cannon; the State House bell, the Christ Church chimes, and St. Peter's bell, eleven in all, were carried off at night under the appearance of farm wagons. The Liberty Bell rode on his wagon, drawn by his own horses, with his eleven-year-old son beside him. The Moravian diary at Bethlehem records on September 23, 1777, that “the wagon with the State House Bell broke down here, so it had to be unloaded,” and neighbor Frederick Leaser's wagon finished the haul to Zion Reformed Church in Allentown, where the bell lay under the floor until June 1778. Pastor Abraham Blumer, who helped hide it, later became father-in-law to his daughter Sarah. He was killed by a tree falling on him near his home at Mickleys, December 12, 1808. A plaque at Zion Reformed (1908) and a family memorial at St. John's Union Cemetery (1917) commemorate him.

### Joseph J. Mickley (1799–1878)

**Place:** Catasauqua → Philadelphia  
**Role:** “Father of American numismatics”  
**Generation:** 5  
**Status:** relative

Joseph Jacob Mickley, born March 24, 1799, at Catasauqua, son of John Jacob III, the boy on the bell wagon. Went to Philadelphia in 1811, apprenticed as a piano maker, and built up his own instrument business, taking a Franklin Institute prize in 1831. From 1842 to 1869 his house at 927 Market Street was a gathering place for collectors, historians, and musicians. He assembled the finest coin collection in America of its day, with autographs of the signers of the Declaration and every president to Grant; a burglary on April 13, 1867, led him to sell most of the coins at auction that autumn. First president of the Numismatic and Antiquarian Society of Philadelphia, founded 1857. Fluent in French, German, and Swedish, he toured the mints of Europe from 1869 to 1872 gathering specimens for the United States Mint, and wrote on the Indian raids in Pennsylvania (1875). Died in Philadelphia February 15, 1878.

- Parents: Pvt. John Jacob Mickley III
- Spouses: Cordelia Hopfeldt, m. 1825; Diana Blumer
- Children: Six, incl. Henry Joseph, Josephine, Hannah, John Jacob (1836–1892)

### Prof. Karl Ludwig Michelet (1801–1893)

**Place:** Berlin  
**Role:** Hegelian philosopher; compiled the Metz family table  
**Generation:** 5  
**Status:** relative

Born in Berlin December 4, 1801, son of Louis Guillaume Michelet (1775–1841) and Wilhelmine Victoire Girard, of the Huguenot French colony. Doctor of philosophy at Berlin in 1824, professor from 1829 until his death; a pupil of Hegel who edited Hegel's collected works (1832–42), founded the Berlin Philosophical Society in 1845, and wrote on ethics, Aristotle, and the personality of God. In 1883, answering a letter from Minnie F. Mickley of Pennsylvania, he sent the genealogical table of the Michelets of Metz from 1444, reasoned out that her ancestor Jean Jacques must be the missing eldest son of Louis Michelet and Suzanne Mangeot born at Zweibrücken, and concluded that the American and Berlin families descended from two brothers. Her father visited him in Berlin in 1889. Died December 15, 1893, the year the Mickley genealogy was printed.

## Millet / Millot

### Sgt. Frederick Millet (Mylot, Miloth) (fl. 1780–82)

**Place:** Colebrookdale Twp., Berks Co., PA  
**Role:** Tailor; militia sergeant, 1780 and 1782  
**Generation:** 6  
**Status:** direct ancestor of Chris Millet; link marked probable

A tailor taxed in Colebrookdale Township, Berks County, in 1781 (“Mylot, Fred'k, taylor”), the only person of the name in the county's surviving tax lists of 1767–1785. Served twice as a sergeant of the Berks County militia during the Revolution: in Captain Henry Strouch's company of Colonel Joseph Hiester's Sixth Battalion, on active duty from August 10 to September 9, 1780, and in Captain Abraham Keefer's company mustered September 10, 1782, where his fellow sergeants were Abraham Lesher of the Oley ironmaking family and Michael Kooser. Both companies were raised from the Colebrookdale and Oley district south-east of Reading: the Kussers, Eckerts, Mattes, and Yoxheimers on their rolls are his neighbors on the Colebrookdale list. Colebrookdale and Oley were settled in part by Huguenot families, and the Millot/Milot name is French. He is not found in the 1790 or 1800 census indexes under any spelling, and no Millot appears in the Brunswick Township (Orwigsburg) lists of 1779–1785, so the family's move north came later. Four people of this rare surname, born 1777 to about 1785 in the same county, fit as his children: Maria (1777) and George (1782), who stayed in Oley, and John (about 1781) and Martin, who were in Orwigsburg by 1815. None of these links is yet proved by a baptism or will. FamilySearch's full-text index of Berks and Schuylkill deeds and probate records contains no Frederick Millot, Milot, Mylot, or Miloth; the baptisms, if they survive, are in the Oley and Colebrookdale church registers of the 1770s and 1780s, which are not indexed online.

### John Millot (c. 1781–bef. 1830)

**Place:** Orwigsburg, Schuylkill Co., PA  
**Role:** Independent Republican committeeman, 1820  
**Generation:** 5  
**Status:** relative; link marked probable

Married Elizabeth Noaker (Noecker) about 1810; their five children were baptized at Zion's “Old Red” Union Church near Orwigsburg between 1811 and 1817. Held lot 201 in Orwigsburg with Frederick G. Fidler from 1815, sold his share in June 1815, and joined Fidler in conveying the lot to Martin Millot in September 1816; bought lot 74 at a sheriff's sale for $2.25 as highest bidder about 1819. His own affairs went badly: a three-acre tract of his in Brunswick Township, adjoining Wendel Seib and John Featherman, was advertised for sheriff's sale in August and September 1818, and in May 1820 the sheriff advertised his half lot on the Centre turnpike in Orwigsburg, 52 feet 6 inches wide and 11 perches deep, with a two-story frame dwelling and stable, bounded by John Kelly and Jacob Reinheimer, to be sold at Michael Graeff's inn on June 19, 1820, “late the estate of John Millot”; the neighboring lot of John Dell had gone the same way that March. He was active in the Hiester party all the same: at the Independent Republicans' Fourth of July celebration at Christian Deibert's spring in 1819 a Mr. Millot, John or Martin, gave the toast “Independent Republicanism expects every man to do his duty in the moment of trial” to six cheers, and at the county meeting of August 19, 1820, held at the Orwigsburg court-house to back General Joseph Hiester for governor, John Millot and John Kolb were named the committee of vigilance for the borough. Head of a household in Orwigsburg in the 1820 census, gone by 1830. Elizabeth afterward married Joseph Snyder and died January 15, 1876, buried at St. John's, Orwigsburg. The lot transactions tie him to Martin, and the two are most likely brothers.

## Nickerson

### William Nickerson (1604–1690)

**Place:** Norwich, England → Chatham, MA  
**Role:** Founder of Chatham  
**Generation:** 11  
**Status:** direct ancestor of Chris Millet

Born in England in 1603 or 1604; a weaver of Norwich, Norfolk. Married Anne Busby there on June 24, 1627, and had four children in Norwich. Puritan Congregationalists, the family left to escape religious persecution: the Family Association's history has them sailing on April 15, 1637, with Anne's parents Nicholas and Bridget Busby and about a hundred others, on either the Rose or the John and Dorothy, two ships under a father and son, and landing at Salem on June 20. Five more children were born around Boston. By 1640 they were among the first settlers of Yarmouth on Cape Cod. In 1656 he bought about a thousand acres, four square miles of Monomoit, from the sachem Mattaquason for a shallop, ten coats, six kettles, twelve axes, twelve hoes, twelve knives, forty shillings in wampum, a hat, and twelve shillings in coin. He had not asked Plymouth Colony's leave, and sixteen years of dispute followed before he settled the matter in 1672 for ninety pounds. About 1664, aged about sixty, he built his homestead near the head of Ryder's Cove, deeded land to his grown children, who settled around the cove with their families in low cottages banked with dried seaweed, and held church services in his own house, the beginning of Chatham's Congregational church; in William Smith's phrase, for the next twenty-five years the place was “but little more than a Nickerson neighborhood.” He wanted the constablewick made a town, which required a resident minister and the population to support one; his children and grandchildren saw it done on June 11, 1712, when Monomoit was incorporated as Chatham. He and Anne lived in the homestead until their deaths in the late 1680s. By 1790 twenty of Chatham's 193 households were Nickersons; by 1915 his descendants were reckoned at fifty thousand.

Savage, consulted in September 2026, adds detail to the crossing and conflicts with the Family Association on two points. He has William as a weaver of Norwich, aged 33 in 1637 — a birth about 1604, as the tree already carries — with his wife Ann, eldest daughter of Nicholas Busby, aged 28, and the four children who crossed with them now named: Nicholas, Robert, Elizabeth, and Ann. He says they embarked at Ipswich or Yarmouth on 8 April 1637, not 15 April, and landed on 20 June at Boston, not Salem; that they went first to Watertown with Nicholas Busby; that William was admitted freeman on 2 May 1638; and that at Yarmouth he had a son Joseph, born December 1647, and a daughter Elizabeth who married Robert Eldred in October 1649. Where Savage and the Association disagree, neither has been checked against a primary record here.

### Nicholas Busby

**Place:** Norwich, England → New England  
**Role:** Crossed in 1637  
**Generation:** 12  
**Status:** direct ancestor of Chris Millet

A weaver of Norwich and father of Anne Busby Nickerson. With his wife Bridget he joined his daughter's family for the 1637 crossing, one of the eight in the Nickerson party. Savage, who had not been consulted before September 2026, carries the rest of his life: he came from old Norwich, landing 20 June 1637 aged 50 — a birth about 1587 — with Bridget, 53, and four children, Nicholas, John, Abraham, and Sarah. He settled first at Watertown, was made freeman in March 1638, served as selectman in 1640 and 1641, removed to Boston in 1646, was constable there in 1649, and died 28 August 1657.

His will, dated 25 July 1657, gave “all his books of physic” to his eldest son John, then in England, and his books of divinity to Abraham. It names his eldest daughter Ann, wife of William Nickerson of Boston, weaver; his daughter Catharine Savory; his youngest daughter Sarah, wife of John Grout of Sudbury and before that of Capt. Thomas Cakebread; and his grandson Joseph, son of his son Nicholas, who had died before him. A man leaving separate libraries of physic and divinity was a reader of some means, which is the only sense of his standing the records give.

The will itself has not been read here. Everything in the paragraph above rests on Savage's 1860 summary of it and should be checked against the Suffolk County probate record.

### Anne Busby Nickerson (1607–1686)

His death is fixed only by the letters of administration of July 18, 1864.

Rice, are not online; John Millot's children (1811–1817) are known from that compilation via FamilySearch, which suggests Martin's children may be there too. Phase 2 confirmed that FamilySearch's indexed church records hold no Millot baptism in Schuylkill County for 1800–1830 at all, John's children included, so their absence proves nothing; the register images themselves (Ancestry's Pennsylvania church and town records, or FHL microfilm) are the next step.

The usgwarchives transcriptions of St. John's Orwigsburg were unreachable.
- **Martin Millot after 1830.** No Millot, Millet, or Millett household in Schuylkill County in the 1840 census index under any spelling; he may be indexed as Miller. No Find a Grave memorial for Martin or Catharine. The Martin Millett who bought a lot in Mahanoy City in the 1860s is an Irish-born man of a different family; the Martin Millot naturalized in Schuylkill County in 1856 is presumably the same immigrant.

- **Zweibrücken.** No indexed baptism for Jean Jacques Michelet (1697). The church books are held by the Zentralarchiv der Evangelischen Kirche der Pfalz in Speyer and are partly on Archion; the 1883 letter from Berlin says the Protestant records of the period were probably lost.

## The Jean-François Millet question

Family lore links the Millets to the painter Jean-François Millet (1814–1875). There is no evidence for it and the timeline argues against it. The painter was born at Gruchy, Gréville-Hague, Normandy, to Jean-Louis-Nicolas Millet (1791–1835); his grandfather was Marin Nicolas Millet (1750–1813), and the family's tree runs back through Norman farmers to a Jean Millet born in 1706, all in La Hague. Chris's line was already in Pennsylvania before the painter's father was born, spelled Millot, Mylot, and Miloth until the 1840s. Any connection would have to lie in France before about 1750, where the Pennsylvania family's origin is unknown. Sources: Britannica; Geneastar family tree of Jean-François Millet; Musée d'Orsay.

## Open leads, in rough order of promise

1. **Nicholas Busby's will, Suffolk County probate, 25 July 1657.** Everything now recorded of his life after 1637 comes from Savage's 1860 summary of this will. The original would confirm the six children, the two libraries, Sarah's two marriages, and the grandson Joseph — and is the single document that most repays being read.
2. **Salem or Boston, and 8 or 15 April 1637.** Savage and the Nickerson Family Association disagree on both the port of arrival and the sailing date. The passenger list of the *Rose* or the *John and Dorothy*, or Winthrop's journal for June 1637, should settle it.
3. **Nicholas Miloth, ship Lydia, 1749 — Sgt. Frederick's likely father.** Egle's oath-of-allegiance lists hold one `Nickolaus Miiloth` (OCR), last of 108 names on the Lydia, Capt. John Randolph, from Rotterdam, qualified 13 October 1749 — the only name of the kind in the volume, and matching Frederick's own spelling in the 1782 muster. An oath-taker was sixteen or older, so a birth by 1733, which fits a father for a man taxed as a tailor in 1781. To settle it: **Strassburger's *Pennsylvania German Pioneers* vol. II**, which reproduces the signatures for this list, and then **Berks County estate and Orphans' Court records for a Nicholas Miloth, c. 1750–1785**. Not entered in the tree until the signature is read.
4. **Marie Violet Williams's birth mother.** A Schuylkill County marriage of William Earl Williams about 1920–21, and a death or divorce before April 1930; then the 1920 census of Gilberton or Tamaqua for the couple. This is the sharpest remaining gap close to the present.

## Method and tools

## Phase 3 log (September 7, 2026)

1. **Sgt. Frederick Millet, deeper search.** *Pennsylvania Archives* Third Series vol. XVIII was read in full: it holds exactly one Millot of any spelling, `Mylot, Fred'k, taylor,` under Colebrookdale, Berks County, 1781, with no valuation against his name — a landless artisan. The 1790 census was then searched for the whole of Pennsylvania rather than Berks alone, confirming his absence and turning up an unrelated-looking Millott cluster in Bedford County. Egle's oath-of-allegiance lists yielded the one candidate above him: **Nickolaus Miiloth**, last of 108 names on the ship Lydia, Capt. John Randolph, from Rotterdam, qualified 13 October 1749. The spelling matches Frederick's own in the 1782 muster. It rests on a single OCR pass of a single scan, could not be corroborated in a second scan or in Strassburger, and is **not** entered in the tree; see Open leads.
2. **Savage's *Genealogical Dictionary*.** All four volumes searched. Volume I gave Nicholas Busby's age, trade, offices, death, and the will of 25 July 1657 naming six children and a grandson — filling what had been the largest gap on that side. Volume III gave William Nickerson's age and trade, named the four children who crossed in 1637, and put the landing at Boston on 20 June rather than Salem, with embarkation on 8 April rather than 15. Volume II supplied the Eldred, Hedges, and Grout cross-references. Volume IV yielded nothing usable: Mercy Williams is unfindable among the Williamses, and Dorcas Covel (b. 1714) falls past Savage's 1693 cutoff.
3. **People added.** Nicholas Busby Jr., John Busby, Abraham Busby, Catharine Busby Savory, and Sarah Busby Grout as Anne's siblings; Elizabeth Nickerson Eldred and Ann Nickerson Hedges as her daughters. Six of the seven crossed in 1637 and carry the event marker in the tree.
4. **Source texts kept.** The OCR of every volume searched is now in `sources/` in the repository, with a README recording what each one settled.
