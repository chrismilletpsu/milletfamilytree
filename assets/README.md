# Research images

Page images of the documents this research was read from, kept so a reading can be
checked against the thing itself rather than against a transcription. Each folder
below says where its images came from, what is in them, and whether they are
committed to the repository or held only on this machine.

Two rules govern what is committed. The repository is public and auto-deploys, so
anything here is published. Page images of an out-of-copyright printed volume,
cited as evidence, are committed. Images produced by a subscription service from
its own microfilm are not, however freely they may be viewed by a subscriber.

## `pa-archives-5th-ser-v5/` — committed

*Pennsylvania Archives*, Fifth Series, vol. 5 (1906) — the Berks County militia
rolls. archive.org item
[`pennsylvania-archives_1906_5_series-5`](https://archive.org/details/pennsylvania-archives_1906_5_series-5).
Leaf index runs 1 ahead of the printed page, calibrated on leaf 254 = p. 253.

| File | Printed page | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `p242-strouch-company-1780-frederick-millet.jpg` | 242 | Pay roll of Capt. Henry Strouch's company, Sixth Battalion, Col. Joseph Hiester, 10 Aug – 9 Sep 1780. Sergeants: Christopher Berninger, Andrew Haas, **Frederick Millet** |
| `detail-p242-sergeants-1780.jpg` | 242 | The officers and sergeants, enlarged |
| `p287-keefer-company-1782-frederick-miloth.jpg` | 287 | Pay roll of Capt. Abraham Keefer's company, 1782: **Frederick Miloth, Sergt., entered Sept. 10, service 1 mo. 14 das.** |

What these settled: the 1782 roll gives his term of service, which had not been
recorded — one month and fourteen days, the same as Michael Kooser and the
lieutenant, where the captain, the ensign, Abraham Lesher and both corporals
served one month and twenty-three days. More importantly, the two rolls spell him
differently. The 1782 roll has *Miloth*, as expected. **The 1780 roll has
"Frederick Millet"** — the modern spelling, sixty years before the notes had the
family adopting it. Caveat worth keeping: this is the 1906 printed transcription,
not the clerk's manuscript, and a final *-ot* and *-et* are easy to confuse in an
eighteenth-century hand, so it may be the editors' reading rather than the
original. Either way, "Millet only after the 1840s" can no longer be asserted
flatly, and `RESEARCH.md` has been corrected in the three places it said so.

| `p421-lydia-list-142c.jpg` | 421 | The list, continued |

| `detail-p422-moloth-in-list.jpg` | 422 | The name in its column, enlarged |

What these settled: the editors read the umlaut too, so *Möloth* is not my reading
of the facsimile alone; the name is printed **without** the mark-cross that stands
beside neighbours who could not write, attesting his literacy a second way; the
ship's people came from Württemberg, Durlach, **Zweibrücken** and the Palatinate;
and a sweep of the whole volume for every spelling of the name returns this one
hit, in fifty years of Philadelphia arrivals. It also closed a lead: **no captain's
list survives for the Lydia**, so his age cannot be recovered, and whether he was
Sgt. Frederick's father or grandfather must be settled from Berks County records.

What these settled: the `Nickolaus Miiloth` that Egle's OCR gave is a real man who
signed his own name, so he was literate; he wrote it *Möloth*, a German
orthography rather than a French *Millot*; and the list qualified on 9 October
1749, not the 13th. See the Sgt. Frederick Millet entries in `RESEARCH.md`. The
signature still cannot be joined to Frederick by any document.

Strassburger's OCR text is deliberately *not* kept in `sources/` — see the note
there. The cited page images from both volumes are a different thing from
republishing a three-volume work, which is why these are committed and that is not.

## `suffolk-probate-1657-busby-will/` — **local only, gitignored**

The will of **Nicholas Busby of Boston**, dated 25 July 1657, proved 14 October
1657. Suffolk County (Massachusetts) Probate Records, *Probate Records, Vol 1–4,
1628–1667*, at volume pages 308–311, with marginal references to the original
volume at pp. 291, 295 and 296.

Read on Ancestry, *Massachusetts, U.S., Wills and Probate Records, 1635–1991*,
[collection 9069](https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/9069), film
`007703070`, images 166–168 of 832. A subscription is needed to reach them.

Note what these images are. The hand is a uniform nineteenth-century roundhand
running across every will in the book, John Gallop's as well as Busby's, and the
attestation reads "By me Nicholas Busby & a seale" — the copyist recording that
the original bore a signature and seal. This is therefore the **record copy** in
the probate register, not the sheet Busby signed. It is authoritative for the
text and it is what Savage worked from, but it is not an autograph.

What these settled: the first primary record tying the Busbys to the Nickersons.
Busby calls William Nickerson "my sonne" and Anne Nickerson "my Eldest daughter",
which fixes the marriage from the father's side. The transcribed passages are in
`RESEARCH.md`.

To reach them again, open the collection above and go to film `007703070`,
image 166; the viewer's Share → Download gives the full-resolution plate.
